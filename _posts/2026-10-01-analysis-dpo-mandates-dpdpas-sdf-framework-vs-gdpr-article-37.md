As India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDPA) and its accompanying Rules 2025 solidify the nation's privacy landscape, businesses are keenly evaluating their compliance obligations. A critical aspect for many entities, particularly those handling substantial volumes of personal data, is the role of the Data Protection Officer (DPO). While the DPDPA introduces an equivalent function for 'Significant Data Fiduciaries' (SDFs), its framework presents notable distinctions when compared to the well-established DPO requirements under the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) Article 37.

## The Mandate for a Data Protection Officer

Under the DPDPA, the obligation to appoint a Data Protection Officer primarily falls upon a 'Significant Data Fiduciary'. Section 10(2) of the DPDPA mandates that an SDF must appoint "an officer who shall be based in India." The criteria for designating an entity as an SDF are outlined in Section 10(1), which empowers the Central Government to prescribe these based on factors such as the volume and sensitivity of personal data processed, the risk of harm to Data Principals, potential impact on national sovereignty and security, and the need for public order. The DPDPA Rules 2025 further elaborate on these specific thresholds and indicators. This framework grants the Indian government considerable discretion in determining which entities fall under the SDF category, potentially encompassing a wide array of organisations across sectors.

In contrast, GDPR Article 37(1) sets out more specific triggers for mandatory DPO appointment. These include public authorities or bodies, and organisations whose core activities involve either large-scale, regular and systematic monitoring of data subjects, or large-scale processing of special categories of data (e.g., health data, racial origin) or data relating to criminal convictions and offences. While both regimes aim to identify high-risk processing, the DPDPA's SDF designation, with its broader governmental discretion, could potentially lead to a wider range of entities being mandated to appoint a DPO compared to GDPR's more narrowly defined criteria. This makes the DPDPA potentially stricter in its scope of mandatory DPO appointments depending on how the SDF criteria are applied.

## Role, Qualifications, and Organisational Standing

The DPDPA outlines specific requirements for the appointed Data Protection Officer. Section 10(2)(a) stipulates that this officer must be "key managerial personnel or an equivalent serving in that capacity" and "responsible to the Board of Directors or similar governing body of the Significant Data Fiduciary." Furthermore, the officer must be "based in India." This emphasis on the DPO being a key managerial figure, directly accountable to the highest governing body, underscores a high level of internal authority and integration within the organisation's leadership structure. The DPDPA Rules 2025 are expected to detail further qualifications, such as relevant experience or certifications.

GDPR, under Article 37(5), requires the DPO to possess "expert knowledge of data protection law and practices." Article 38(3) mandates that the DPO "shall directly report to the highest management level" and "shall not be dismissed or penalised by the controller or the processor for performing his or her tasks." It also ensures the DPO operates independently and is not subject to instructions regarding the exercise of their tasks. While DPDPA's requirement for the DPO to be a Key Managerial Personnel (KMP) implies a senior standing, GDPR provides more explicit protections for the DPO's independence and job security, making it stricter in safeguarding the DPO's autonomy. The DPDPA is currently silent on explicit protections against dismissal or penalties for performing DPO duties, and while a KMP role suggests influence, it doesn't inherently guarantee the same level of independence from internal pressures as GDPR's explicit provisions.

## Functions and Oversight Mechanisms

The DPDPA assigns clear functions to the Data Protection Officer of an SDF. Section 10(2)(b) specifies that the officer will act as the point of contact for the grievance redressal mechanism under the Act and for the Board of Directors. Their role also encompasses advising the Board on compliance with the DPDPA. A unique and significant feature of the DPDPA is the additional requirement under Section 10(3) for an SDF to appoint an "Independent Data Auditor" to carry out periodic audits of its policies and practices. This auditor is distinct from the DPO and provides an external layer of scrutiny.

GDPR Article 39 details the DPO's tasks, including informing and advising the controller/processor and employees about their obligations, monitoring compliance, providing advice regarding Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs), and cooperating with the supervisory authority. The DPO also serves as a contact point for data subjects and the supervisory authority. Both frameworks assign core advisory and liaison responsibilities to the DPO. However, the DPDPA's introduction of an Independent Data Auditor for SDFs is a *stricter* and additional oversight mechanism not directly paralleled in GDPR's DPO framework. This external audit function could potentially augment the DPO's internal monitoring efforts, providing an independent verification of compliance.

## Practical Takeaway

For Indian businesses, especially those anticipating designation as Significant Data Fiduciaries, understanding these nuances is crucial. While both the DPDPA and GDPR aim to embed data protection expertise within organisations, the Indian regime places a strong emphasis on the DPO's senior managerial status and local presence. Businesses must not only identify suitable KMP to fulfil this role but also consider how the DPO's responsibilities will interface with the mandatory Independent Data Auditor. Unlike GDPR, where the DPO often leads all aspects of compliance monitoring, the DPDPA introduces a dual-layered oversight that requires careful coordination. Organisations should proactively assess their data processing activities against the anticipated SDF criteria, prepare for the appointment of an India-based KMP as DPO, and establish clear lines of communication and responsibility between the DPO, the Board, and the Independent Data Auditor to ensure robust and compliant data governance.
