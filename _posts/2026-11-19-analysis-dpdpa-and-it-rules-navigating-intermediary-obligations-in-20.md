As we stand in November 2026, India's digital landscape is firmly shaped by two pivotal pieces of legislation: the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 (IT Rules 2021), and the fully operational Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDPA). For intermediaries, particularly those designated as Significant Social Media Intermediaries (SSMIs), this dual regulatory framework presents a complex, yet critical, compliance challenge. The DPDPA has fundamentally shifted how personal data is handled, overlaying a new layer of accountability onto the existing obligations under the IT Rules.

## The Evolving Landscape for Intermediaries

The IT Rules 2021, initially introduced to ensure due diligence by intermediaries, primarily focus on content regulation, user safety, and grievance redressal mechanisms. They mandate a set of duties for intermediaries, including publishing terms of service and privacy policies (Rule 3(1)(i)), removing unlawful content upon notification (Rule 3(1)(b)), and appointing a Grievance Officer (Rule 3(2)). For SSMIs, additional obligations such as traceability (Rule 4(2)) and proactive content monitoring (Rule 4(4)) were introduced, largely addressing issues of misinformation, hate speech, and national security.

The DPDPA, however, approaches digital governance from a different angle: the protection of individual privacy. It designates entities processing personal data as "Data Fiduciaries" (Section 2(j)) or "Data Processors" (Section 2(k)), imposing stringent obligations related to consent, data minimisation, purpose limitation, and security. Many intermediaries, by their very nature of collecting and processing user data, fall squarely within the definition of a Data Fiduciary. This means that beyond managing content and user conduct, they must now meticulously manage personal data in line with the DPDPA's principles, fundamentally altering their operational paradigm.

## Overlapping Responsibilities and Potential Conflicts

The interplay between these two laws creates both complementary and potentially conflicting requirements. For instance, the IT Rules 2021 require intermediaries to publish a privacy policy (Rule 3(1)(i)). The DPDPA elevates this significantly, mandating explicit, informed, and unambiguous consent from Data Principals for processing personal data (Section 6(1)). This consent must be specific to the purpose, and Data Principals have the right to withdraw it at any time (Section 7). An intermediary's generic privacy policy under IT Rules might not suffice for DPDPA's granular consent requirements, necessitating a more robust and dynamic consent management framework.

Grievance redressal also presents an area of overlap. The IT Rules 2021 require the appointment of a Grievance Officer to address user complaints related to content and platform conduct (Rule 3(2)). The DPDPA, particularly for Significant Data Fiduciaries (SDFs), mandates the appointment of a Data Protection Officer (DPO) (Section 13(1)) and establishes a mechanism for Data Principals to register grievances concerning their personal data (Section 13(2)). While the Grievance Officer handles content-related issues, the DPO focuses on data protection matters. Intermediaries must ensure these roles are either integrated or closely coordinated to provide a seamless redressal experience for users.

A more challenging area lies in data retention and traceability. The IT Rules 2021, particularly for SSMIs, implicitly or explicitly require data retention for compliance purposes, such as identifying the "first originator" of information (Rule 4(2)). However, the DPDPA champions purpose limitation and storage limitation (Section 5(2)), stipulating that personal data should not be retained beyond the period necessary to fulfil the specified purpose or as required by law. Reconciling the need for data retention under IT Rules with the DPDPA's principles of data minimisation and deletion presents a significant compliance tightrope for intermediaries.

## Enhanced Accountability for Significant Data Fiduciaries

A crucial aspect of the DPDPA is the designation of "Significant Data Fiduciaries" (SDFs) (Section 10), which will undoubtedly include many SSMIs. This designation triggers a host of additional, stringent obligations. SDFs must not only appoint a DPO (Section 13(1)) but also undertake Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) (Section 11) and engage independent data auditors (Section 12). These requirements mirror global best practices, such as those seen in the EU's GDPR, where DPIAs are mandatory for high-risk processing activities. For an SSMI, conducting a DPIA on its data processing activities will be essential to identify, assess, and mitigate risks to Data Principals, ensuring compliance with both the DPDPA and the spirit of responsible digital governance.

The DPO, in particular, will become the linchpin for compliance, advising the intermediary on its DPDPA obligations, monitoring internal compliance, and acting as a point of contact for the Data Protection Board of India (DPBI) and Data Principals. This role extends beyond the scope of the IT Rules' Grievance Officer, demanding deep expertise in data protection law and practices.

## Harmonization and Future Outlook

The Indian regulatory landscape is still maturing, and the DPDP Rules (expected to be notified soon) will provide much-needed clarity on the practical implementation of the DPDPA. The Data Protection Board of India (DPBI), established under Section 18, will play a critical role in interpreting the law, issuing guidelines, and enforcing compliance. While the IT Rules 2021 and the DPDPA address different facets of digital governance – content and platform conduct versus personal data protection – their ultimate goal is to foster a safer and more trustworthy digital ecosystem. Intermediaries must view these laws not as separate silos but as interconnected frameworks that together define their responsibilities towards users.

**Practical takeaway:**
Indian businesses operating as intermediaries, particularly GCs and DPOs, must undertake a comprehensive audit of their current operations against both the IT Rules 2021 and the DPDPA. Prioritise establishing robust, granular consent mechanisms (Section 6(1)) and review data retention policies to align with purpose and storage limitations (Section 5(2)). Integrate or closely coordinate the functions of the Grievance Officer and the DPO (Section 13(1)) to streamline user redressal. Invest significantly in enhanced security safeguards (Section 8(5)) and develop clear data breach notification protocols (Section 19). For SSMIs likely to be designated as SDFs, proactively conduct Data Protection Impact Assessments (Section 11) to identify and mitigate data risks, embedding privacy-by-design principles into all new and existing services.
